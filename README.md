## **5 Key Takeaways**

1. **Price is the only starting point.**
   Scarcity is an industry fact. A stock is a claim on future cash. Once the bottleneck is visible, you earn only what the market has not already paid for. Reverse-engineer the operating state the price requires, then test it against history and physics.

2. **Micron at $927 demands permanent ~70% DRAM gross margins.**
   Best year ever was 58.9%. Near-term EPS (even above consensus) is almost irrelevant—the entire valuation rests on the post-shortage steady state. Capital-heavy commodity businesses rarely hold margins 9–11 points above their own record once capacity arrives.

3. **The real question is duration, not existence.**
   Mild shortage (TrendForce) needs 70%. Deep, multi-year shortfall (SK Group view) needs only mid-50s to low-60s. The stock is pricing the extreme case. Independent evidence for that extreme case is still thin.

4. **Who captures the rent matters more than the shortage itself.**
   PJM is short capacity, yet a regulatory collar and backstop procurement leave only ~1% of Vistra equity value per year of post-collar outcome. Scarcity value does not automatically flow to existing equity holders.

5. **Peak multiples are noise.**
   6× forward looks cheap only because those are peak earnings. On the normalized NOPAT the price itself requires, it is 19×. Always re-anchor to the steady-state economics the market is forcing you to underwrite.
